# Bài đọc: Đời sống hằng ngày

## Moving to a New Neighborhood

When Anna moved to a new neighborhood last spring, she knew almost no one. Her apartment was small but bright, with a large window that looked out over a quiet street lined with old trees. For the first few weeks, she mostly kept to herself, unpacking boxes and figuring out where the nearest grocery store was.

Everything changed one Saturday morning when her neighbor, an elderly man named Mr. Tran, knocked on her door holding a plate of homemade spring rolls. "Welcome to the building," he said with a smile. "I noticed you moved in a few weeks ago, but I didn't want to bother you while you were still settling in."

That small gesture made a bigger difference than Anna expected. Over the following months, she started greeting other neighbors in the hallway, joined a weekend walking group that met in the nearby park, and even began attending a monthly potluck dinner organized by residents on her floor. What had once felt like an unfamiliar, slightly lonely place gradually became somewhere she genuinely looked forward to coming home to.

Looking back, Anna realized that the hardest part of moving wasn't finding a new apartment or learning her way around — it was being willing to step outside her comfort zone and let people in.

## Từ vựng nổi bật

| Từ/cụm | Nghĩa | Loại từ |
|---|---|---|
| neighborhood | khu phố, khu dân cư | n. |
| settle in | ổn định (nơi ở mới) | phrasal verb |
| gesture | cử chỉ (thể hiện tình cảm) | n. |
| unfamiliar | xa lạ, chưa quen | adj. |
| comfort zone | vùng an toàn (tâm lý) | n. phrase |
| potluck dinner | bữa ăn mỗi người mang một món | n. phrase |

## Câu hỏi đọc hiểu

1. How did Anna feel during her first few weeks in the new neighborhood?
2. What did Mr. Tran do to welcome Anna?
3. Name two things Anna started doing after meeting her neighbor.
4. According to Anna, what was the hardest part of moving?

<details>
<summary>Đáp án</summary>

1. She felt somewhat isolated/lonely and mostly kept to herself.
2. He brought her a plate of homemade spring rolls and welcomed her to the building.
3. She started greeting neighbors, joined a weekend walking group, and attended a monthly potluck dinner (any two).
4. Being willing to step outside her comfort zone and let people in — not the practical parts of moving.
